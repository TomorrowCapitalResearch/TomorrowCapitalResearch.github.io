Our approach

A question.
A model. A test.

Careful observation, explicit assumptions and experiments that can be repeated. Progress comes from what survives scrutiny.

01

A repeatable
process

Each result can send us back to the question.

  1. 01 / Observe

    Define the question.

    Specify the phenomenon, the available information and the conditions under which an explanation could be wrong.

  2. 02 / Formalise

    Make the idea precise.

    Choose measurable quantities, state the assumptions and establish a simple baseline before adding complexity.

  3. 03 / Test

    Challenge the explanation.

    Use independent evaluation, alternative models and sensitivity checks. In market research, include costs, liquidity and the information available at decision time.

  4. 04 / Build & review

    Put useful findings to work.

    Develop reproducible tools, document their limits and keep reviewing them as the underlying system changes.

A closer look

Give the idea
an independent test.

Historical observations help develop a model. Later, unseen observations help assess whether it generalises. Keep that boundary clear.

Process schematic, not a prescribed split or a result. The evaluation design depends on the data, horizon and dependence between observations.

02

What we
look for

Traceable evidence

Sources, transformations, decisions and assumptions that can be inspected.

Meaningful comparisons

Simple baselines and alternative explanations that test the value of complexity.

Practical conditions

Constraints and implementation details that matter to the actual decision.

Room to revise

Clear limits, changing conditions and results that may challenge the original idea.
